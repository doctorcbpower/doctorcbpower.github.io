Research

My research sits around a few connected questions: what dark matter is, how it shapes galaxies together with ordinary baryonic physics, and how reliably any of that can be recovered from simulations and observations. The topics below are related rather than separate — most of what I do touches more than one of them.

Dark matter

I am interested in the nature of dark matter, and in what observations of galaxies and large-scale structure can actually tell us about it. This includes work across different dark matter models — cold, warm, self-interacting and other alternatives — and the connection between small-scale structure and the galaxies we observe. I am less interested in producing a single constraint than in understanding how robust that constraint is to the assumptions and modelling choices that sit between the underlying physics and what we measure.

Galaxy formation and dynamics

Dark matter and baryonic physics act together to determine the structure and dynamics of galaxies. I work on questions of galaxy and halo structure, disc stability, baryonic contraction, and how simulated galaxies compare with observed ones.

Cosmological simulations

Much of this work is done with large cosmological simulations, including zoom simulations of individual haloes. I am interested both in using simulations to answer scientific questions and in the numerical and computational methods that make those calculations tractable in the first place.

Computational astrophysics and scientific infrastructure

I like building things that other researchers can use: simulation infrastructure, scientific software, reproducible computational workflows, and tools that make complicated calculations easier to inspect and extend. This extends to high-performance and GPU computing, and to broader Australian computational-science infrastructure and community efforts I have been involved in.

Scientific inference

A question that runs through most of the above is where information is lost along the chain from fundamental physics, through simulation and galaxy formation, to what we actually observe and infer. Working out which assumptions matter, and what a given observation can genuinely constrain, is as much a part of the work as any individual result.
